# SP Digital BPR Survey Analysis

This repository demonstrates Excel data skills by converting qualitative survey responses into a coded quantitative dataset and producing analysis-ready outputs.

## Project overview
I collected open-text survey responses from 31 participants about departmental business processes, bottlenecks, and improvement opportunities. I coded themes, cleaned the data, and used Excel to create pivot tables, charts, and a concise summary of findings to support business process reengineering (BPR).

## Files
- SP_Digital_BPR_Survey_Raw.xlsx — original survey export (text responses).
- SP_Digital_BPR_Survey_Clean.xlsx — cleaned and coded workbook with sheets: Raw, Cleaned, PivotTables, Charts, Summary.
- SP_Digital_BPR_Survey_Clean.csv — CSV export of the cleaned dataset.
- Analysis/PivotTables.xlsx — reusable pivot tables.
- Analysis/Charts/*.png — exported chart images used in the summary.
- Codebook.md — mapping of qualitative themes to numeric codes.
- methodology.md — step-by-step description of coding and validation.

## How I converted qualitative to quantitative
1. Reviewed all open-text responses and identified recurring themes.
2. Built a codebook mapping themes to numeric codes and sentiment labels.
3. Applied codes in the Cleaned sheet and validated with spot checks.
4. Created pivot tables and charts to show frequency, sentiment distribution, and top bottlenecks.

## Key outputs
- Frequency table of top business processes mentioned.
- Distribution of common bottlenecks and delays.
- List of repetitive tasks suitable for automation.
- Sentiment summary by question.
- Visuals: bar charts, stacked charts, and pivot-driven dashboards.

## How to view
1. Open `SP_Digital_BPR_Survey_Clean.xlsx` in Excel.
2. Inspect the `PivotTables` sheet and `Charts` folder for visual summaries.
3. Read `Codebook.md` and `methodology.md` to understand coding decisions.
